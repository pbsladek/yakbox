# The Relay Test

At midnight, three technicians gathered in the relay room for the final systems test. Rain moved across the high windows, and status lights painted the concrete walls in slow bands of green and amber. The narrator handles this paragraph because it has no speaker directive.

The first technician watched a clean waveform settle across the central display. The second stood beside the emergency cutoff, certain that the harmless test signal had begun answering questions nobody had asked. At the back of the room, the third technician compared both readings and waited for the argument to begin.

“Keep the relay open,” the first technician said. “We finally have a stable response, and shutting it down now would erase the only evidence we have.”

<!-- yakbox:speech:speaker name=character-2 -->

“That response is following us between channels,” the second technician replied. “A stable signal is not the same thing as a safe one.”

The waveform doubled. Every dark monitor in the relay room woke with the same measured pulse. For a moment, none of the technicians moved, and the rain against the windows sounded louder than the machinery.

“Both of you are reading intention into a diagnostic pattern,” the third technician said. “Give me thirty seconds to isolate the source before either of you touches the controls.”

<!-- yakbox:speech:speaker name=character-1 -->

“We have already given it thirty seconds,” the first technician said. “Each time we wait, it becomes more precise.”

The second technician rested one hand beside the cutoff without pressing it. The third adjusted the receiver by a fraction, separating the main pulse from a quieter rhythm beneath it.

<!-- yakbox:speech:speaker name=character-2 -->

“If the lower rhythm changes again, I am ending the test,” the second technician said. “Evidence will not matter if we cannot contain what produced it.”

<!-- yakbox:speech:speaker name=character-3 -->

“Then listen instead of arguing,” the third technician said. “The second rhythm is not following the relay. It is counting us.”

The final tone arrived. Three measured pulses crossed the display, followed by three more. Then the relay went silent, and every status light in the room changed from green to red.
